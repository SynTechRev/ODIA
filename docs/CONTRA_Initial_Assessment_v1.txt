SECTION 2 — CASI SCORING FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

The Commercial Adhesion Severity Index (CASI) is ODIA's quantitative asymmetry
measure for consumer contracts. It aggregates five axes, each scored 0-20:

  Axis 1 — Procedural Adhesion          (P)
    Degree to which the consumer has no meaningful ability to negotiate terms.
    Indicators: take-it-or-leave-it language, e-sign-only execution, no
    paper alternative, auto-acceptance on continued use.

  Axis 2 — Modification and Consent     (M)
    Unilateral modification rights held by the entity without consumer consent
    other than continued use. Indicators: "we may change at any time" clauses,
    no notice requirement, retroactive applicability.

  Axis 3 — Enforcement Cost Asymmetry   (E)
    Structural imbalance in dispute resolution costs. Indicators: mandatory
    arbitration, class-action waiver, fee-shifting against consumers, choice-
    of-law clauses designating advantageous jurisdictions.

  Axis 4 — Remedy Foreclosure           (R)
    Contractual elimination of consumer remedies. Indicators: damages caps,
    consequential-damage waivers, indemnification of entity by consumer,
    injunctive-relief-only limitations.

  Axis 5 — Data Extraction Depth        (D)
    Scope and permanence of data collection, retention, and third-party
    sharing rights. Indicators: broad collection grants, indefinite retention,
    sale/sharing to undefined third parties, cross-service data pooling.

CASI Aggregate = sum of five axes (0-100).

  Band                Aggregate Range    Interpretation
  ----                ---------------    --------------
  Baseline Adhesion       0 - 20         Standard commercial adhesion; consumer
                                         rights largely intact.
  Elevated Asymmetry     21 - 40         Notable power imbalance; specific
                                         clauses merit individual scrutiny.
  Substantial Asymmetry  41 - 60         Systematic asymmetry across multiple
                                         axes; consumer remedies materially
                                         constrained.
  Severe Asymmetry       61 - 80         Compounding asymmetry; multiple
                                         foreclosure mechanisms active
                                         simultaneously.
  Foreclosure Regime     81 - 100        Near-total remedy foreclosure.
                                         Consumer contractual rights
                                         functionally eliminated.

SECTION 6 — CROSS-ENTITY PATTERNS
================================================================================

6.1 The Foreclosure Architecture
---------------------------------
Six documents achieve Foreclosure Regime status (CASI 81-100). All six are
ToS-type instruments. None are privacy notices. This pattern is consistent
with the hypothesis that the arbitration-and-waiver foreclosure mechanism
is embedded in the service agreement layer, while the privacy notice layer
operates at a lower (Substantial/Elevated) asymmetry level by handling a
different foreclosure function: data extraction rights.

The full foreclosure model therefore operates in two layers:
  Layer 1 (ToS)          — Forecloses dispute resolution and remedies
  Layer 2 (Privacy)      — Forecloses data sovereignty rights

Both layers operate simultaneously. A consumer who signs an AT&T master ToS
(CASI 84) and is subject to AT&T's privacy practices (CASI 25-26) faces
compounding foreclosure across both layers.

6.2 The Regulatory Compliance Baseline
---------------------------------------
The corpus consistently shows that documents produced primarily for regulatory
compliance purposes (CCPA annual reports, HIPAA notices, nondiscrimination
notices, e-sign disclosures, arbitration initiation forms, subpoena processing
policies) cluster in the Baseline Adhesion band (CASI 0-20). This is the
expected result and serves as an internal calibration point: the CASI scoring
system correctly distinguishes between regulatory-compliance documents and
operative consumer agreements.

6.3 The EULA Asymmetry Inversion
----------------------------------
EULAs — historically considered the archetype of take-it-or-leave-it consumer
agreements — score in the mid-Elevated to Severe range in this corpus (26-62),
not in the Foreclosure tier. The highest-scoring EULA (AT&T eBonding, CASI 62)
is a business-to-business-facing instrument. Consumer EULAs score 26-28. This
inversion suggests the EULA format, while structurally adhesive, does not
commonly incorporate the full remedies-foreclosure stack (mandatory arbitration
+ class waiver + damages cap + indemnification) that drives ToS documents
into the Foreclosure Regime.

6.4 Data Volume vs. CASI Score Divergence
-------------------------------------------
The Chase Digital Services Agreement (529 findings, CASI 84) and Google's
current Privacy Policy (119 findings, CASI 44) illustrate that finding count
and CASI score are related but distinct measures:
  - High CASI + High findings: Chase DSA — pervasive asymmetry, broad scope
  - Moderate CASI + High findings: Google Privacy — concentrated asymmetry,
    very broad scope with extensive data-use cataloguing
  - High CASI + Moderate findings: Comcast Web Services ToS (74 findings,
    CASI 88) — efficient foreclosure architecture; fewer clauses, each more
    extreme

The Google privacy policy finding-count increase from 26 (2008) to 117 (2026)
without a corresponding CASI increase suggests that Google has expanded the
scope of its data practices (more services, more categories, more third parties)
while maintaining the same structural power distribution established in 2008.
This is consistent with growth of the Alphabet product portfolio rather than
any change in the foundational privacy architecture.

SECTION 8 — ANALYTICAL FLAGS FOR OPUS ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

The following observations are presented as hypotheses for Opus to assess,
refute, or develop. They are derived from the quantitative CASI data and
structured findings but require legal-reasoning and contextual analysis
beyond what the automated pipeline produces.

Flag 1 — AT&T Template Propagation
  Three AT&T ToS documents score CASI 84 (master ToS, Quantum Fiber, U-Verse).
  A fourth (2022 version) scores CASI 86. The identical scores across legally
  distinct product agreements (wireless, broadband, TV) suggest a shared
  drafting template rather than independent instruments. If confirmed, this
  means a single template decision propagates maximum asymmetry across all
  AT&T product lines simultaneously. Question for Opus: Is there a basis for
  a single-document challenge to cover all AT&T products, or does the product-
  line separation require separate proceedings?

Flag 2 — Google CASI 44 Lock and GDPR
  Google's privacy policy has maintained CASI 44 through GDPR implementation
  (May 2018 onward). The May 2018 version (CASI 44) is post-GDPR. This either
  means: (a) GDPR's consent requirements were absorbed without reducing the
  structural asymmetry measured by CASI, (b) CASI does not capture GDPR-
  relevant dimensions adequately, or (c) Google's GDPR compliance operates
  primarily through the cookie consent mechanism rather than through changes
  to the core privacy policy architecture. Question for Opus: Does the CASI
  44 stability post-GDPR represent a genuine finding about GDPR's consumer-
  protection reach, or a limitation of the CASI methodology?

Flag 3 — Chase DSA and Zelle Instrument Stacking
  A consumer who opens a Chase checking account and uses Zelle is simultaneously
  bound by: Chase Digital Services Agreement (CASI 84, 529 findings), Zelle
  Privacy Notice (CASI 40, 63 findings), Zelle E-Sign Disclosure (CASI 26,
  8 findings), and Zelle Site Terms (CASI 58, 25 findings). The consumer's
  effective asymmetry exposure is not the maximum of these scores but the
  union of all distinct foreclosure mechanisms across all instruments. No
  single CASI score captures this. Question for Opus: How should stacked-
  instrument total exposure be characterized in legal terms — is it an
  additive burden or a categorical one?

Flag 4 — Kaiser HIPAA Notice Scoring Substantial
  A HIPAA-mandated disclosure document scoring CASI 48 (Substantial Asymmetry)
  merits specific attention. HIPAA's Notice of Privacy Practices has regulated
  content requirements, but the Substantial score suggests Kaiser has added
  material beyond the minimum. Specifically relevant: what language in Kaiser's
  NCAL HIPAA notice drives the score above the regulatory-baseline threshold
  that CCPA annual reports and nondiscrimination notices score at? Question for
  Opus: What is the likely regulatory basis for challenging non-mandatory
  language in a HIPAA-required disclosure, and does the HIPAA "minimum
  necessary" standard apply to the notice itself?

Flag 5 — Comcast Privacy Policy Finding Density
  Comcast's Xfinity Privacy Policy scored 156 findings against CASI 49. The
  finding-to-score ratio (approximately 3.2 findings per CASI point) is the
  highest in the corpus. This suggests the privacy policy contains many
  individual disclosures of asymmetric practices that collectively score
  Substantial but individually each is modest. This pattern is consistent
  with a "hiding in plain sight" data-practice architecture — extensive data
  use disclosed in granular detail, each piece defensible alone, the aggregate
  structurally imbalanced. Question for Opus: Does disclosure of comprehensive
  data practices — even in granular form — satisfy the spirit of CCPA/CPRA
  transparency requirements when the aggregate effect is structural asymmetry?

SECTION 9 — METHODOLOGY NOTES
================================================================================

9.1 Detector Suite
------------------
Each document was processed through the full C.O.N.T.R.A. detector pipeline:
L-11 through L-20 (10 commercial-contract detectors). All detectors are pure
functions; results are deterministic given identical input text.

9.2 Text Extraction
-------------------
PDF text extracted via pdfplumber (primary) with pypdf fallback. Some PDFs
produced "Ignoring wrong pointing object" warnings from malformed internal
PDF structure (specifically AT&T and Comcast documents with non-conformant
cross-reference tables). Text extraction succeeded for all 144 documents
despite these warnings — the warnings are from the PDF parser's object
resolution, not from text extraction itself.

9.3 Deduplication
-----------------
Two documents were deduplicated at ingest time via SHA-256 hash:
  - Comcast Acceptable Use Policy (1).pdf = Comcast Acceptable Use Policy.pdf
  - google_terms_of_service_en_us_second.pdf = duplicate of another Google ToS
  Both were caught correctly. 32 additional documents are flagged needs_review
  due to low doc-type inference confidence; these require manual classification
  before inclusion.

9.4 Source Tier
---------------
All 144 ingested documents are T1 (direct entity-published source). No Wayback
Machine captures were used in this initial ingest (fetch_wayback=False during
batch run, disabled for speed). Wayback capture for historical versions can be
enabled on a per-document basis in subsequent runs.

9.5 Limitations
---------------
  - No jurisdiction-specific analysis in this assessment. CASI scores do not
    reflect California-specific consumer protections (CCPA/CPRA, Cal. Civ.
    Code 1750 et seq.) that may modify the effective asymmetry for California-
    resident users.
  - No cross-instrument stacking analysis performed. Individual document scores
    do not capture the compounding effect of multiple simultaneous agreements.
  - Document set is initial corpus only. 31 of 36 catalogued entity directories
    remain empty. Scores will shift as the corpus grows.
  - Version-chain linkage has not been implemented. Documents with the same
    entity and document type are not yet explicitly linked as predecessor/
    successor. The version_label and effective_date fields enable this linking
    in future analysis.
