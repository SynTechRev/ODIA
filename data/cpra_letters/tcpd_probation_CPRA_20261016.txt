────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 1 — JAG/EDWARD BYRNE GRANT COMPLIANCE (34 U.S.C. § 10152)
           [60 documents identified — CRITICAL priority]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A review of Tulare County BOS records identified 60 or more documents referencing
TCPD's receipt or use of Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant (JAG)
funds without the anti-supplanting certification required under 34 U.S.C.
§ 10152(a)(1)(G) and the Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) JAG Program Guide.
Anti-supplanting requires that federal JAG funds supplement — and not replace —
state and local funding for law enforcement activities.

Please provide:

  1a. All JAG award letters, sub-award agreements, and Memoranda of Understanding
      (MOUs) accepted by TCPD from the U.S. Department of Justice / BJA for
      grant years 2006 through 2026.

  1b. The anti-supplanting certification submitted with each JAG application or
      award acceptance for grant years 2006 through 2026.

  1c. All JAG programmatic and financial progress reports (SF-PPR and SF-425
      or equivalent) submitted to BJA for grant years 2006 through 2026.

  1d. All equipment, technology, and personnel expenditures funded wholly or
      partially with JAG funds, for grant years 2006 through 2026.

  1e. Any BJA monitoring reports, audit findings, or correspondence regarding
      TCPD's JAG compliance, for the same period.

  1f. All BOS resolutions, staff reports, and agenda items related to TCPD JAG
      grant acceptance, expenditure authorization, or compliance reporting.

REQUEST 2 — SURVEILLANCE TECHNOLOGY WITHOUT BOS AUTHORIZATION
           [7 documents — CRITICAL governance findings]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Seven documents in BOS records reference TCPD surveillance capabilities without
any corresponding BOS resolution, agenda authorization, or public hearing record,
in potential conflict with AB 481 (Gov. Code § 7070 et seq.) and general
separation-of-powers requirements for county department equipment authorization.

Please provide:

  2a. All BOS resolutions, agenda items, and staff reports authorizing TCPD to
      acquire or operate any surveillance technology, including ALPR systems,
      body-worn cameras, drones/UAS, facial recognition, or predictive policing
      tools, from January 1, 2018 through the date of this request.

  2b. TCPD's AB 481 Militarized Equipment Use Policy, all annual reports
      submitted to the BOS under AB 481, and all BOS resolutions approving
      or renewing that policy, for fiscal years 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25,
      and 2025-26.

  2c. The AB 481 equipment inventory disclosed in each annual report, including
      any ALPR systems, drones/UAS, less-lethal weapons, or other surveillance
      or enforcement equipment.

REQUEST 3 — ALPR SYSTEM (SB 524 / Civil Code § 1798.90.5)
           [3 critical + 3 high documents]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Three Board documents reference TCPD's operation of or access to an Automated
License Plate Reader (ALPR) system without citation to an SB 524 AI-transparency
impact assessment, and three reference ALPR without a Civil Code § 1798.90.5
usage and privacy policy.

Please provide:

  3a. All contracts, purchase orders, amendments, and data-sharing agreements
      related to any ALPR system used by or accessible to TCPD, including
      agreements with vendors such as Flock Safety, Vigilant Solutions,
      or any county-wide ALPR network, from January 1, 2018 through the
      date of this request.

  3b. TCPD's Civil Code § 1798.90.5 ALPR usage and privacy policy, including
      the date of adoption and any public-facing posting location.

  3c. The SB 524 (Stats. 2023, ch. 833) AI-transparency impact assessment, or
      equivalent documentation, prepared before TCPD deployed or accessed any
      ALPR system or AI-assisted surveillance tool.

  3d. Any data-sharing agreements between TCPD and federal agencies (including
      ICE, FBI, or HSI) providing access to ALPR data or TCPD surveillance
      records.
