Dear Public Records Coordinator:

Pursuant to the California Public Records Act (CPRA), Government Code §§ 7920.000
et seq., I respectfully request the following public records from the Fresno County
Sheriff's Office (FCSO). This request arises from a review of Fresno County Board
of Supervisors records — a corpus of 32,340 legislative documents — identifying
surveillance technology deployments and federal grant activities that lack required
statutory compliance documentation under current California law.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 1 — ALPR SYSTEM (SB 524 / Civil Code § 1798.90.5)
           [20 critical + 20 high documents — ALPR without statutory citation]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Twenty or more documents in BOS records reference Fresno County's use of or access
to Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) technology without citation to the SB 524
AI-transparency impact assessment, and twenty without citation to a Civil Code
§ 1798.90.5 ALPR usage and privacy policy. One document references Flock Safety
specifically as an ALPR vendor.

Please provide:

  1a. All contracts, purchase orders, amendments, and data-sharing agreements
      related to any ALPR system used by or accessible to FCSO, including
      agreements with vendors such as Flock Safety, Vigilant Solutions,
      or any county-wide or regional ALPR network, from January 1, 2018 through
      the date of this request.

  1b. FCSO's Civil Code § 1798.90.5 ALPR usage and privacy policy, including
      the date of adoption and any public-facing posting location.

  1c. The SB 524 (Stats. 2023, ch. 833) AI-transparency impact assessment, or
      equivalent documentation, prepared before FCSO deployed or accessed any
      ALPR system or AI-assisted surveillance tool.

  1d. Any data-sharing agreements between FCSO and federal agencies (including
      ICE, DEA, FBI, or HSI) providing access to ALPR data or FCSO surveillance
      records.

  1e. FCSO's ALPR audit logs for calendar year 2025, including query counts,
      hit rates, and authorized access records, to the extent disclosable
      under Civil Code § 1798.90.5.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 2 — BODY-WORN CAMERA AND AXON PROGRAM (CJIS COMPLIANCE)
           [76 documents with Axon Enterprise reference; 71 BWC/CJIS gap docs]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Seventy-six Board documents reference Axon Enterprise Inc. as a vendor for
body-worn cameras, Tasers, or cloud analytics services, and 71 reference a
body-worn camera program without citation to FBI Criminal Justice Information
Services (CJIS) Security Policy compliance.

Please provide:

  2a. All contracts, purchase orders, amendments, and service orders with Axon
      Enterprise, Inc. for body-worn cameras, Taser devices, Evidence.com cloud
      storage, Draft One AI report-writing, Fleet vehicle cameras, or any other
      Axon product or service, from January 1, 2015 through the date of this
      request.

  2b. FCSO's current body-worn camera policy, including data retention, access
      controls, evidentiary upload procedures, and any reference to CJIS
      Security Policy compliance.

  2c. The most recent CJIS Security Policy compliance audit, assessment, or
      self-certification applicable to FCSO's BWC data storage, Evidence.com
      access, and any cloud-hosted law enforcement data.

  2d. FCSO's SB 524 AI-transparency documentation (or equivalent) for any
      AI-assisted tool in the Axon ecosystem, including Axon Draft One
      AI report-writing software.

  2e. FCSO's current Axon Draft One usage policy, including officer-verification
      requirements before submission of AI-generated report content.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 3 — FACIAL RECOGNITION (AB 1215)
           [8 documents with facial recognition / AB 1215 findings]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Eight documents in BOS records reference facial recognition technology in
connection with FCSO activities. AB 1215 (Gov. Code § 832.19) prohibited the use
of facial recognition technology in connection with body-worn cameras from
January 1, 2020 through January 1, 2023. Subsequent legislation governs any
post-2022 use.

Please provide:

  3a. All contracts, MOUs, or data-sharing agreements related to facial recognition
      technology, facial matching systems, or biometric identification systems
      used by or accessible to FCSO, from January 1, 2018 through the date
      of this request.

  3b. FCSO's written policy on the use of facial recognition technology,
      including any restrictions, approval requirements, and audit logging.

  3c. Any records of FCSO's use of facial recognition technology or biometric
      identification systems in operational law enforcement activities, including
      any use connected with body-worn cameras, from January 1, 2020 through
      the date of this request.

REQUEST 4 — DRONE / UAS PROGRAM (AB 481)
           [12 documents with drone/UAS without AB 481 language]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Twelve documents in BOS records reference FCSO drone or UAS operations without
citation to an AB 481 Militarized Equipment Use Policy or annual report.

Please provide:

  4a. FCSO's current drone/UAS Militarized Equipment Use Policy under AB 481
      (Gov. Code § 7070 et seq.), including BOS approval date.

  4b. FCSO's AB 481 annual reports for fiscal years 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25,
      and 2025-26, together with the BOS resolutions approving each.

  4c. All FAA authorizations (Certificates of Authorization or Part 107 waivers)
      and all contracts for drone/UAS hardware, software, or maintenance,
      from January 1, 2018 through the date of this request.

  4d. Any logs or records of drone/UAS operations conducted by FCSO, including
      mission type, duration, and geographic area, for calendar year 2025.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 5 — JAG/EDWARD BYRNE GRANT COMPLIANCE (34 U.S.C. § 10152)
           [FCSO component of 190 total JAG-deficient BOS documents]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Board records identified 190 or more documents referencing JAG funds for County
law enforcement activities — including FCSO procurements — without the anti-
supplanting certification required under 34 U.S.C. § 10152(a)(1)(G). Nineteen of
those documents also reference surveillance vendors alongside JAG funding, raising
an additional JAG-funded technology itemization issue.

Please provide:

  5a. All JAG award letters, sub-award agreements, and MOUs under which FCSO
      has received or administered JAG funds, for grant years 2006 through 2026.

  5b. The anti-supplanting certification submitted with or alongside each FCSO
      JAG award acceptance.

  5c. All itemized lists of equipment, technology, or personnel funded wholly
      or partially with JAG funds, including any surveillance or ALPR technology,
      for grant years 2006 through 2026.

  5d. Any BJA monitoring reports, audit findings, or corrective action plans
      applicable to FCSO's JAG activities.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 6 — LEXIPOL POLICY PLATFORM (94 documents)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Ninety-four documents in BOS records reference the Lexipol California State Master
policy platform as the source or template for FCSO policies — including use-of-force,
surveillance, and technology policies. Lexipol is a vendor-supplied boilerplate;
BOS approval of "policy updates" without specifying the substantive changes creates
a governance gap.

Please provide:

  6a. FCSO's most recent Lexipol California State Master policy manual or policy
      library, in full, as currently in effect.

  6b. All BOS agenda items, staff reports, and resolutions approving or ratifying
      FCSO policy updates (including Lexipol quarterly or annual policy bundles)
      for fiscal years 2018-19 through 2025-26.

  6c. Any internal FCSO analysis, legal review, or deviation documentation
      showing where FCSO policy departs from the Lexipol California template
      for surveillance, use-of-force, or data-retention provisions.
