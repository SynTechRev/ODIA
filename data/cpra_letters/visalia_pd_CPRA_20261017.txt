Dear Records Division:

Pursuant to the California Public Records Act (CPRA), Government Code §§ 7920.000
et seq., I respectfully request the following public records from the Visalia Police
Department (VPD). This request arises from a review of VPD public records — a corpus
of 340 documents — identifying surveillance technology deployments that lack required
statutory compliance documentation under current California law. Specifically, 22
documents in the VPD corpus reference Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR)
technology associated with Flock Safety, LLC, producing 355 anomaly findings across
Civil Code § 1798.90.5, SB 524 AI-transparency, and AB 481 compliance layers. Flock
Safety represents the highest single-vendor anomaly density in the VPD corpus
reviewed, and 75 of those findings flag cross-entity ALPR detections without the
SB 524 AI-transparency citation required since January 1, 2024.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 1 — FLOCK SAFETY ALPR CONTRACTS AND COMPLIANCE DOCUMENTATION
           (Civil Code § 1798.90.5 / SB 524 / AB 481)
           [22 documents — Flock Safety reference / 355 findings]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Twenty-two documents in the VPD corpus reference Flock Safety, LLC as an ALPR
vendor. None of the 22 documents contain a citation to VPD's Civil Code § 1798.90.5
ALPR usage and privacy policy, and none cite an SB 524 (Stats. 2023, ch. 839)
AI-transparency impact assessment. AB 481 (Gov. Code § 7070 et seq.) language
appears inconsistently — present in a subset of documents but absent from others
referencing the same deployment — suggesting a governance gap in the equipment-use
policy adoption process.

Please provide:

  1a. All contracts, purchase orders, amendments, sole-source justification
      memoranda, and renewal options between VPD (or the City of Visalia on
      VPD's behalf) and Flock Safety, LLC, for ALPR system procurement,
      installation, maintenance, or data services, from January 1, 2018
      through the date of this request.

  1b. VPD's current ALPR usage and privacy policy required by Civil Code
      § 1798.90.5, including the date of adoption, the date of most recent
      update, and any public-facing posting location.

  1c. The SB 524 (Stats. 2023, ch. 839) AI-transparency impact assessment, or
      equivalent documentation, prepared before VPD deployed or accessed any
      Flock Safety ALPR system or AI-assisted surveillance tool, including any
      third-party AI components integrated into the Flock Safety platform.

  1d. VPD's AB 481 (Gov. Code § 7070 et seq.) Militarized Equipment Use Policy
      as approved by the Visalia City Council, specifically identifying ALPR
      systems as a covered technology category, along with the BOS/City Council
      resolution adopting the policy.

  1e. VPD's AB 481 annual reports for fiscal years 2022-23, 2023-24, 2024-25,
      and 2025-26, together with any City Council resolutions ratifying them,
      to the extent those reports address ALPR system deployment.

  1f. All data-sharing agreements, network participation agreements, or
      interoperability agreements authorizing VPD to share ALPR data with,
      or access ALPR data from, any other agency — including but not limited
      to the City of Fresno, Fresno County Sheriff's Office, TCSO, or any
      regional or statewide ALPR network administered by Flock Safety, LLC.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 2 — ALPR AUDIT LOGS AND DATA RETENTION (Civil Code § 1798.90.5(b))
           [75 cross-entity ALPR detection findings / SB 524 gap lattice]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Seventy-five cross-entity ALPR detection findings in the VPD corpus flag records
where ALPR hits were acted upon or documented without the AI-transparency citation
required by SB 524 since January 1, 2024. Civil Code § 1798.90.5(b) requires ALPR
operators to maintain audit logs of all ALPR data access and to make those logs
available to authorized parties.

Please provide:

  2a. VPD's ALPR data retention schedule, including the retention period for
      raw ALPR images, associated vehicle data, and plate-read event records,
      and the legal authority cited for that schedule.

  2b. VPD's ALPR audit logs for calendar years 2024 and 2025, including query
      counts by accessing officer/unit, hit counts, and records of any data
      shared with or received from other agencies, to the extent disclosable
      under Civil Code § 1798.90.5.

  2c. All documented instances in which VPD ALPR data was shared with a federal
      agency (including ICE, DEA, FBI, or HSI), including any requests received
      and the legal authority cited for compliance or denial.

  2d. Any privacy impact assessment or internal policy review conducted in
      connection with VPD's adoption of Flock Safety's cross-jurisdictional
      "hot list" or alert-notification features.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 3 — CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL SURVEILLANCE NETWORK DISCLOSURE
           (AB 481 / SB 524 / Civil Code § 1798.90.5)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The cross-entity ALPR findings in the VPD corpus indicate that VPD's Flock Safety
deployment is networked with at least one other agency in the region. Neither
interoperability agreements nor cross-agency data-access policies are reflected in
available VPD records.

Please provide:

  3a. All MOUs, data-sharing agreements, or participation agreements between VPD
      and any other law enforcement agency, school district, private entity, or
      regional body governing access to a shared Flock Safety ALPR network or
      "hot list" system.

  3b. Any public notice, community engagement record, or City Council agenda
      item in which VPD disclosed its participation in a regional or multi-
      agency Flock Safety ALPR network to the public.

  3c. Any written legal analysis or policy determination regarding VPD's
      obligation to adopt a separate AB 481 policy for shared ALPR access
      (as distinct from VPD-operated ALPR systems), consistent with the
      California Department of Justice's AB 481 guidance.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REQUEST 4 — JAG / FEDERAL GRANT FUNDING FOR SURVEILLANCE TECHNOLOGY
           (34 U.S.C. § 10152)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Federal JAG grants carry an anti-supplanting prohibition under 34 U.S.C.
§ 10152(a)(1)(G): grant funds may not replace local funds already committed to
the same purpose. If any component of VPD's Flock Safety ALPR contract was
funded through JAG or other federal grants, the anti-supplanting certification
and itemization requirements apply.

Please provide:

  4a. All JAG award letters, sub-award agreements, and MOUs under which VPD
      has received or administered JAG funds for any purpose, from grant years
      2006 through 2026.

  4b. Any itemized equipment or technology lists submitted with JAG grant
      applications or award certifications, including any entries for ALPR
      systems, body-worn cameras, or other surveillance technology.

  4c. The anti-supplanting certification submitted with or alongside each
      VPD JAG award acceptance.
